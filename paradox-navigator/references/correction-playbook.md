# Correction Playbook: Micro-Corrections for All 12 Polarities

This playbook provides specific, actionable micro-corrections for each of the 12 founder polarities. Each correction includes concrete actions, not principles. Use this playbook when you have identified that you are over-indexing on one pole and need to shift toward the other.

---

## 1. Speed vs. Quality

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Speed

**Action 1: Implement a Bug Bash**
- Schedule a 2-hour session before any customer-facing release
- Gather 3-5 team members who did not build the feature
- Have each person use the feature as a customer would
- Document all bugs found and prioritize fixes before launch
- Owner: QA Lead

**Action 2: Add One Automated Test Per Feature**
- For every new feature shipped, require one automated test in the critical path
- This creates a gradually expanding safety net without slowing initial velocity
- Integrate test creation into the feature definition, not as afterthought
- Owner: Engineering Lead

**Action 3: Create a "Quality Week" Sprint**
- One sprint per quarter with zero new features
- Entire engineering team focuses on tech debt, tests, and reliability
- Publish the improvements made to stakeholders
- Owner: CTO

**Action 4: Require Design Review for High-Visibility Features**
- Any feature visible to more than 20% of users requires design review
- Design review checks for consistency, usability, and polish
- Limit review to 30 minutes per feature
- Owner: Head of Design

**Action 5: Introduce a "Clean Code" Rotation**
- Engineers spend 10% of sprint capacity on code quality
- Rotate focus areas each sprint to avoid stagnation
- Track code quality metrics to show improvement over time
- Owner: Engineering Manager

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Quality

**Action 1: Set an Aggressive Launch Date**
- Pick a date that feels uncomfortably soon
- Write it down and share it publicly
- Hold the date as non-negotiable; negotiate scope instead
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 2: Reduce Feature Scope by 50%**
- For the next release, cut the feature list in half
- Ship half the features, but ship them faster
- Use the remaining scope as a follow-on release
- Owner: VP of Product

**Action 3: Create a Minimum Viable Spec Template**
- One page maximum for any feature spec
- Must include: user problem, success metric, must-have features only
- Anything beyond one page requires Head of Product approval
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 4: Limit Design Review to 30 Minutes**
- Timer starts when review begins
- At 30 minutes, make a binary go/no-go decision
- Document any issues found for future iteration, do not block launch
- Owner: Head of Design

**Action 5: Ship to 5% Cohort First**
- Launch to a small group of friendly users first
- Set a 48-hour observation period
- If no critical bugs, expand to full launch
- Owner: Engineering Lead

## 2. Focus vs. Exploration

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Focus

**Action 1: Allocate Exploration Capacity**
- Reserve 10% of sprint points for exploratory work
- Do not assign exploration stories to specific outcomes
- Review exploration results monthly
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 2: Conduct Adjacent Customer Interviews**
- Interview 5 customers per quarter who are outside your core segment
- Ask about problems you do not currently solve
- Document insights in a shared "opportunity register"
- Owner: Customer Success Lead

**Action 3: Run a Quarterly Opportunity Review**
- 2-hour session to review market developments
- Each team member presents one potential opportunity
- Vote on whether to explore any new directions
- Owner: CEO

**Action 4: Create an Idea Parking Lot**
- Anyone can submit ideas to the parking lot
- Review parking lot monthly
- Celebrate "parking lot graduates" that become initiatives
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 5: Define Minimum Market Size**
- Set a threshold: no strategic initiative unless TAM > $500M
- This forces focus on genuine opportunities, not just interesting ideas
- Document exceptions with explicit rationale
- Owner: CEO

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Exploration

**Action 1: Force a Primary Bet**
- Each quarter, explicitly name one primary strategic bet
- All other initiatives are secondary or parked
- Resource allocation must reflect the primary bet (60%+ of capacity)
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Kill Three Initiatives**
- Identify three active initiatives that are not the primary bet
- Make explicit decisions: kill, pause, or hand off
- Communicate decisions to affected teams within one week
- Owner: VP of Product

**Action 3: Implement a Priority Filter**
- New initiatives must pass three criteria: strategic fit, resource availability, competitive timing
- If any criterion fails, initiative goes to parking lot
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 4: Conduct Weekly Focus Audits**
- 15-minute weekly review of team capacity allocation
- Identify work that is misaligned with strategic priorities
- Redirect misaligned work or kill it
- Owner: Engineering Manager

**Action 5: Reduce Active Experiments by 50%**
- Count current active experiments
- Kill or pause half of them immediately
- Focus remaining experiments with clearer success criteria
- Owner: Head of Product

## 3. Build vs. Buy

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Build

**Action 1: Conduct Make-vs-Buy Review**
- For any new infrastructure project, complete a make-vs-buy analysis
- If off-the-shelf exists covering 80%+ of needs, buy is default
- Only build if you have specific, documented competitive advantage
- Owner: CTO

**Action 2: Replace One System with Managed Service**
- Identify the highest-maintenance custom system
- Research managed alternatives
- Execute migration within 90 days
- Owner: Engineering Lead

**Action 3: Create a Build Approval Process**
- Any new build project requires written business case
- Business case must include total cost of ownership over 3 years
- CTO sign-off required for any build decision > 2 weeks of engineering
- Owner: CTO

**Action 4: Benchmark Vendor Costs Against Engineering**
- Calculate fully-loaded cost of internal engineering for equivalent function
- Compare to vendor pricing
- If vendor is cheaper, default to vendor except for core differentiators
- Owner: CFO

**Action 5: Partner for One Non-Core System**
- Identify a non-core system that you have been building
- Find a strategic partner who can provide it as a service
- Negotiate partnership terms within 60 days
- Owner: CEO

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Buy

**Action 1: Identify One Competitive Differentiator**
- Interview top 10 customers about why they chose you
- Find one capability that is core to their decision
- Evaluate whether it depends on a vendor you do not control
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 2: Calculate Total Vendor Cost**
- Sum all vendor costs across the company
- Project costs at current growth rate for 3 years
- Identify the three largest costs and evaluate build alternatives
- Owner: CFO

**Action 3: Assess Vendor Dependency Risk**
- For top 5 vendors, assess: financial health, market position, lock-in risk
- Create a risk register for vendor dependencies
- Develop contingency plans for high-risk dependencies
- Owner: CTO

**Action 4: Explore White-Label Options**
- For core systems provided by vendors, explore white-label or customization
- Negotiate data portability terms in all new vendor contracts
- Owner: CTO

**Action 5: Build Internal Capability for One Critical System**
- Identify the system where vendor limitations are most painful
- Allocate engineering time to develop an internal alternative
- Set a 6-month target for MVP
- Owner: VP of Engineering

## 4. Hire Fast vs. Hire Slow

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Hire Fast

**Action 1: Implement Structured Interviews**
- Create a scorecard with 5-7 specific criteria for each role
- Every interviewer grades each criterion 1-5
- No offer without minimum average score
- Owner: Head of Recruiting

**Action 2: Require Reference Checks**
- All offers require reference checks from two references
- References must answer three standardized questions
- Document reference feedback in candidate file
- Owner: Hiring Manager

**Action 3: Add One Interview Round for Critical Roles**
- Director-level and above require one additional interview
- Additional interview focuses on values and long-term fit
- This adds 3-5 days but catches misalignments earlier
- Owner: Head of Recruiting

**Action 4: Create 90-Day Performance Checkpoint**
- All new hires have formal checkpoint at 90 days
- Checkpoint includes manager review and peer feedback
- Document outcomes; do not ignore red flags
- Owner: Hiring Manager

**Action 5: Track Quality Metrics by Cohort**
- Track first-year performance for each hiring cohort
- Calculate quality scores: performance rating, manager satisfaction, retention
- Use data to calibrate future hiring standards
- Owner: Head of Recruiting

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Hire Slow

**Action 1: Set Maximum Process Length**
- Two weeks maximum from first contact to offer
- Any deviation requires VP approval
- Communicate timeline to candidates upfront
- Owner: Head of Recruiting

**Action 2: Pre-Define Hiring Criteria**
- Hiring criteria must be finalized before opening a requisition
- Criteria cannot change mid-process without VP sign-off
- This prevents scope creep in candidate evaluation
- Owner: Hiring Manager

**Action 3: Implement Panel Interviews**
- Replace sequential interviews with panel format
- One panel interview instead of four individual interviews
- Decision made day-of-panel
- Owner: Head of Recruiting

**Action 4: Authorize Fast-Track for Exceptional Candidates**
- Define "exceptional" criteria in advance (e.g., referral + top 10% benchmark)
- Exceptional candidates get accelerated process
- Limit to 20% of roles to prevent overuse
- Owner: VP of People

**Action 5: Analyze Time-to-Fill vs. Quality Outcomes**
- Compare hire quality for roles that took <30 days vs. >60 days
- If no quality difference, use data to justify faster process
- Owner: Head of Recruiting

## 5. Centralize vs. Distribute

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Centralize

**Action 1: Create a Decision Rights Document**
- Define which decisions are centralized (founder/executive only)
- Define which decisions are distributed (team leads)
- Define which decisions are autonomous (individual contributor)
- Document in shared wiki and revisit quarterly
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Delegate One Decision Per Week**
- Each week, identify one decision you would normally make
- Delegate it to the appropriate team lead
- Provide context but not direction
- Do not override unless safety is at risk
- Owner: CEO

**Action 3: Implement "Default to Autonomy" Principle**
- Any decision that affects only one team's domain is autonomous
- Decisions affecting multiple teams require alignment
- Decisions affecting company strategy require executive input
- Owner: CEO

**Action 4: Establish Forums for Distributed Decisions**
- Create weekly leadership team meeting for cross-team decisions
- Team leads make joint decisions in this forum
- Founder attends only when safety or major resources at stake
- Owner: CEO

**Action 5: Track Decisions by Level**
- Log each decision made and who made it
- Review monthly: count centralized vs. distributed decisions
- Set a target: reduce centralized decisions by 20% quarter-over-quarter
- Owner: COO

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Distribute

**Action 1: Create a Strategic Framework Document**
- One page describing strategic priorities for next 12 months
- Teams must demonstrate how their work connects to framework
- Update framework quarterly
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Implement Monthly Decision Alignment Review**
- Leadership team reviews major decisions from past month
- Identify any decisions that misaligned with strategy
- Course-correct and communicate any needed shifts
- Owner: CEO

**Action 3: Establish Decision Templates**
- For recurring decision types, create templates
- Templates include: context, options considered, criteria, decision made, rationale
- Distribute templates to team leads
- Owner: COO

**Action 4: Audit Three Recent Cross-Team Decisions**
- Review three significant cross-team decisions from past quarter
- Assess: Were the right people involved? Was strategy considered? Was outcome good?
- Adjust process based on findings
- Owner: CEO

**Action 5: Introduce Peer Review for High-Impact Decisions**
- Any decision affecting >$100K or >20% of team requires peer review
- One other team lead reviews before execution
- This catches misalignments before they propagate
- Owner: COO

## 6. Short-Term vs. Long-Term

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Short-Term

**Action 1: Ring-Fence Strategic Capacity**
- 20% of engineering capacity is protected for strategic work
- Strategic work cannot be reprioritized for short-term needs
- Track utilization of protected capacity
- Owner: VP of Engineering

**Action 2: Create Three-Year Scenarios**
- Develop three scenarios for the company at year three
- Include: market position, product capabilities, team size, financials
- Review scenarios quarterly to maintain long-term perspective
- Owner: CEO

**Action 3: Budget for Platform Investment**
- Allocate 15% of annual budget to "platform" line item
- Platform = work that enables long-term capabilities, not short-term features
- Track platform investment vs. feature investment quarterly
- Owner: CFO

**Action 4: Introduce OKRs That Connect Levels**
- Company O → Team O → Individual KR
- Each quarter, explicitly map how team objectives ladder to company vision
- Check-in on connections monthly
- Owner: COO

**Action 5: Hire a Chief of Staff for Strategy**
- Add a Chief of Staff who owns long-term strategic planning
- Chief of Staff creates the framework for long-term thinking
- This keeps long-term perspective in leadership room
- Owner: CEO

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Long-Term

**Action 1: Create Quarterly Landing Zones**
- Define what "good" looks like at end of each quarter
- Landing zones must be achievable and measurable
- Communicate landing zones to all stakeholders
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Identify Minimum Metrics for Investor Confidence**
- Determine the three metrics investors need to see progress
- Set quarterly targets for these metrics
- Hit targets consistently to maintain investor confidence
- Owner: CFO

**Action 3: Set Burn Rate with 18-Month Runway**
- Budget based on 18-month runway, not infinite long-term vision
- If burn rate exceeds this, take action immediately
- This forces long-term thinking to be grounded in reality
- Owner: CFO

**Action 4: Prioritize Near-Term Impact Within Long-Term Vision**
- When evaluating long-term initiatives, prioritize those with near-term benefit
- Long-term work should also create short-term value
- Reject long-term initiatives that only pay off in year 3+
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 5: Communicate Strategy Through Near-Term Milestones**
- Translate long-term vision into 90-day milestones
- Each milestone should be independently valuable
- Celebrate milestone achievements to maintain momentum
- Owner: CEO

## 7. Vision vs. Validation

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Vision

**Action 1: Conduct 10 Customer Discovery Interviews**
- Talk to potential customers with no product in front of them
- Ask about their current solutions and pain points
- Document insights; do not pitch
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 2: Create a Riskiest Assumption List**
- Identify the top 5 assumptions your vision depends on
- For each assumption, design a validation experiment
- Run experiments before building further
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 3: Launch a Landing Page Test**
- Create a landing page describing your vision
- Drive targeted traffic to measure interest
- Track sign-up rates as a proxy for demand
- Owner: Head of Growth

**Action 4: Set a Validation Deadline**
- 12 months from now, you must have evidence of product-market fit
- If evidence is lacking, course-correct or pivot
- This prevents endless vision pursuit without market feedback
- Owner: CEO

**Action 5: Partner with One Believer Customer**
- Find one customer who believes in your vision
- Work closely with them to develop and validate the product
- Use their success as a validation case study
- Owner: Head of Product

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Validation

**Action 1: Schedule Weekly Vision Time**
- One hour per week dedicated to long-term thinking
- No agenda; explore possibilities and imagine futures
- Document insights for future reference
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Interview Non-Customers**
- Talk to people who have not bought from you
- Understand why they have not and what would need to change
- This reveals expansion opportunities
- Owner: Head of Product

**Action 3: Study Category Creators**
- Read biographies and case studies of companies that created categories
- Understand their vision-driven approach
- Extract principles applicable to your market
- Owner: CEO

**Action 4: Create a Possible Futures Document**
- Describe three possible futures for your market in 10 years
- Identify which future you are building toward
- Use this to evaluate current decisions
- Owner: CEO

**Action 5: Run One Vision-Driven Experiment**
- Design one experiment based on your vision, not customer request
- This tests whether your intuition has value
- Evaluate results without confirmation bias
- Owner: Head of Product

## 8. Autonomy vs. Alignment

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Autonomy

**Action 1: Implement Weekly Decision Share**
- Teams present key decisions from the week
- No approval required; just visibility
- This creates accountability without control
- Owner: COO

**Action 2: Create Shared OKR Framework**
- All teams have OKRs that connect to company OKRs
- OKRs are visible to all teams
- Review connections quarterly
- Owner: COO

**Action 3: Establish Cross-Functional Tiger Teams**
- For key initiatives, create cross-functional teams
- Tiger teams have clear mission and decision rights
- This creates alignment without centralizing decisions
- Owner: CEO

**Action 4: Introduce Decision Documentation**
- Major decisions require a one-page decision log
- Include: context, options, decision made, rationale
- Share logs in a central repository
- Owner: COO

**Action 5: Define Three Always-Align Decisions**
- Explicitly list the three decision types that always require alignment
- Examples: pricing changes, new customer segments, major partnerships
- All other decisions default to autonomous
- Owner: CEO

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Alignment

**Action 1: Delegate One Cross-Functional Decision**
- Identify a cross-functional decision that requires alignment
- Delegate it to a team lead with clear authority
- Track the outcome without intervening
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Implement Two-Way Door Framework**
- Categorize decisions: two-way door (reversible) vs. one-way door (irreversible)
- Two-way door decisions get 24-hour turnaround
- One-way door decisions require alignment
- Owner: COO

**Action 3: Set Decision Speed Target**
- Define "slow" as >5 days for any decision
- Track average decision speed monthly
- If exceeding target, identify bottlenecks
- Owner: COO

**Action 4: Reduce Required Stakeholders**
- Audit approval chains for common decisions
- Remove unnecessary approvers
- Target: maximum 2 approvers for any decision
- Owner: COO

**Action 5: Celebrate Fast Autonomous Decisions**
- Recognize teams that made good decisions quickly
- Share case studies of fast decision outcomes
- This reinforces that speed and alignment are not opposites
- Owner: CEO

## 9. Transparency vs. Confidentiality

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Transparency

**Action 1: Classify Information Into Three Tiers**
- Tier 1: Open (all employees)
- Tier 2: Internal (leadership and relevant teams)
- Tier 3: Restricted (founders and specific roles only)
- Document and communicate the tiers
- Owner: COO

**Action 2: Create Confidentiality Guidelines**
- Define what types of information require confidentiality
- Include: customer data, financial projections, M&A discussions, personnel matters
- Train leadership on appropriate sharing
- Owner: COO

**Action 3: Brief Teams on Restricted Decisions**
- When information must be restricted, brief affected teams
- Explain why the information is restricted
- This maintains trust even when sharing is not possible
- Owner: CEO

**Action 4: Implement Need-to-Know Review**
- For board-sensitive topics, require need-to-know review
- Define criteria for who needs to know and when
- This prevents inadvertent over-sharing
- Owner: General Counsel

**Action 5: Conduct Information Audit**
- Quarterly review of what information is being shared and where
- Identify any over-sharing incidents
- Adjust guidelines based on findings
- Owner: COO

### Corrections for Over-Indexing on Confidentiality

**Action 1: Share One Previously Restricted Metric**
- Each month, identify one metric previously shared only with executives
- Share it with a broader audience
- Observe the reaction and adjust
- Owner: CEO

**Action 2: Create Information Accessibility Audit**
- Survey teams on what information they wish they had
- Compare to what they currently receive
- Identify gaps and address them
- Owner: COO

**Action 3: Add Transparency Checkpoint to All-Hands**
- Each all-hands includes a transparency update
- List what was shared this period
- Ask for feedback on what should be shared more
- Owner: CEO

**Action 4: Define Default-to-Open Categories**
- Create a list of information categories that are default-to-open
- Exceptions require explicit justification
- This shifts default behavior
- Owner: COO

**Action 5: Document Information Requests**
- Track requests for information that were denied
- Review denials monthly for pattern
- Identify unnecessary confidentiality
- Owner: COO

## Using This Playbook

### The Micro-Correction Principle

Micro-corrections are small, deliberate adjustments rather than dramatic reversals. The goal is to shift the organization's trajectory by 10-15 degrees, not to make a 90-degree turn. After a micro-correction:

- Observe results for 2-4 weeks
- If trajectory is improving, continue
- If trajectory is not changing, consider additional micro-correction
- If trajectory is over-correcting, pull back slightly

### Frequency of Corrections

Check in on each active polarity:
- Weekly: review trigger conditions and observable signals
- Monthly: assess whether current polarity position is appropriate
- Quarterly: conduct formal polarity review and adjust map

### Combining Corrections Across Polarities

Be cautious about making corrections across multiple polarities simultaneously. Changing too many things at once makes it impossible to know what is working. Prioritize the most urgent polarity and address others sequentially.

### Documenting Corrections

For each correction you implement:
1. What is the specific action?
2. Who is the owner?
3. What is the deadline?
4. What is the expected outcome?
5. What is the review date?

This documentation creates a feedback loop that improves your polarity management over time.
